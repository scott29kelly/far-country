# Vision

## Why Far Country exists

Most Christians can name a handful of images about heaven — streets of gold, gates of pearl, no more tears — but few can describe with any specificity the place the Bible actually portrays. The biblical material on heaven is unusually rich (Genesis to Revelation, with concentrated passages in Isaiah, Ezekiel, the Gospels, Hebrews, and Revelation), but it is scattered, mixed with apocalyptic imagery, and often filtered through inherited assumptions that are more sentimental than scriptural.

**Far Country exists to gather that scattered material into a single, citation-grounded picture, and to make that picture explorable.**

The end-state we are working toward is an environment a user can walk through and ask questions of — a 3D representation of the people, places, and things Scripture says are there, with an AI guide that answers from the dataset rather than from speculation. Not a fantasy. Not a sermon illustration. A faithful, sourced, navigable model.

---

## What this is not

- **Not eschatology lite.** It is not a casual or imaginative take. Every descriptor is sourced.
- **Not an interpretive monolith.** Where Scripture is genuinely debated or symbolic, the dataset preserves the tension rather than resolving it by fiat.
- **Not a virtual worship space or game.** Gamification is not the goal. The 3D layer exists to make the dataset legible spatially, not to entertain.
- **Not a substitute for Scripture.** The app points outward, always — every claim links back to the verse and (where applicable) the page of Willis. The reader is encouraged to verify.

---

## Audience

- **Primary:** lay Christians who want to understand more concretely what the Bible says about heaven, without having to assemble it themselves from scattered passages and a wall of commentaries.
- **Secondary:** pastors, teachers, and students using the dataset as a study aid — citations are first-class so anyone can verify and trace.
- **Tertiary:** the curious. Non-Christians and skeptics who want to see, fairly presented, what Christians actually believe Scripture teaches about heaven.

---

## Theological and philosophical motivation

The Christian tradition has always insisted that heaven is not vague mist but a real country — concrete, embodied (after resurrection), located, populated, ordered. The line from Hebrews 11 is "they desire a *better country*." Augustine called it the City of God. Lewis called it the place where everything in this world that has ever been a hint or a echo finally arrives. MacDonald, Willis, Alcorn — the same impulse, repeatedly: *take the biblical material seriously as describing a real place.*

Far Country is a software-shaped expression of that same impulse. It is built on the conviction that:

1. Scripture's descriptions of heaven are intended to inform, not merely to gesture.
2. Symbolism and literalism both serve that informing — they are tools, not opposites.
3. A faithfully-assembled picture, even if incomplete, is more useful and more honest than a sentimental one.
4. Software can serve formation when it submits to the source material rather than rearranging it.

## What success looks like

In one year:

- A canonical dataset of descriptors of heaven extracted from the ESV and from Willis, every entry sourced, every entry tier-tagged, every entry reviewed by a human.
- A browsable web app where any user can navigate the people, places, and things in the dataset and trace each back to the verse it comes from.
- A grounded Q&A interface that answers questions about heaven and **refuses to answer when the dataset does not support the question**, by design.
- A 3D layer (initial pass, not production-polished) that lets the user walk a representation of the New Jerusalem and inspect entities in space.

In three years:

- The dataset is recognized within the Christian study/teaching community as a high-quality reference.
- The 3D experience is meaningfully usable for devotional or teaching contexts.
- Licensing is in place (ESV API, Willis permission) for public distribution.

---

## Guiding posture

We are not building a thing about heaven. We are building a thing **out of** what Scripture says about heaven. The distinction matters at every level — what enters the dataset, what we are willing to render in 3D, what the AI is permitted to say. The source material constrains us, and that constraint is the point.
